  - _T14.a_ (`d29a0fc9`).
    - Barnes, Lehman and Mulla 2014's improved Priority-Flood (a FIFO pit queue) runs from the sea
      at ℓ and the terminal bodies' pits. A newly closed cell's parent is the cell that closed it
      (the lane's derivation from their Alg. 2), so flats drain in pop order, where Design note 9
      reads "ties by (elevation, cell index)".
    - A receiver is one strictly lower edge neighbour on the filled surface, in proportion to the
      drop per metre, from instance 0; with none, the parent. The slope correction S_r ÷ ‖∇W‖
      (Tzathas et al. §4.3, read from the rendered page) is held at 1 or below, since unequal
      cube-sphere links push it a little above; a receiver whose drops underflow falls back to the
      parent. Since the review the draw is an integer threshold, one `stream.mark()` word picked by
      `Mark::pick_weighted`: the same decision as u < p up to a rounding tie, so no figure moved.
    - Barnes, Callaghan and Wickert 2020's hierarchy is built as the terrain's merge tree (the
      lane's equivalent construction). In their 2021 Fill–Spill–Merge a sea-linked tree spills
      through its oceanlink, latest-linked first; a merge activates whenever both children are full;
      a depression no water fills is a terminal playa; a three-way saddle merges land first.
    - Volume (Q2) and Balance (Q3) take whole cells in fill order by the half-cell rule. **The level
      is the ground of the first cell not taken**, where Q3 reads the last cell's filled level:
      under the millimetre codes that keeps the last cell flooded and one-cell lakes real (accepted
      by main). Water bodies are found once a level, on its construction (matching Q1's fixed
      partition).
