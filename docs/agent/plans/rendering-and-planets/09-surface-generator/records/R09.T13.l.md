  - _GPCP's source._ NOAA PSL's distribution (`precip.mon.ltm.1991-2020.nc`, which T13.e's annual
    zonal table read) could not be reached from the lane's pod, so the tables are computed from the
    same record as NOAA NCEI's GPCP Monthly CDR v2.3 (`v02r03`) distributes it through NOAA's Open

    preliminary file superseded by its final one). Their annual zonal means reproduce T13.e's PSL

    each file's `license` attribute reads "No constraints on data access or use"; NODD's data "are

  - _The temperature's source_ (R09.T13.m, 2026-10-30, in place of the σ = 0.995 lean and its
    heights' reconstruction): `ncep_air_boxes.rs` is NOAA PSL's 2 m long-term means, the data
    `ncep_air.rs` reduces (`air.2m.mon.ltm.1991-2020.nc`, SHA-256 `c86a3c57…a9be9`, with R1's own
    `land.sfc.gauss.nc` and `hgt.sfc.nc`, from the Internet Archive's 2022 snapshot of PSL's former
    path), reduced with R1's mask and heights; banded, they reproduce `ncep_air.rs` to 0.000 K.
    Against the σ = 0.995 table over the land boxes: −0.60 to −0.98 K by month, −0.81 K annually
    (RMS 1.41 K). No assertion moves: each month's r within 0.006, the annual bias +0.02 K (was
    −0.78), the northern land's range 30.0 K and its lag 31.1 days. Main compares the one checksum
    with PSL's current `Monthlies/surface_gauss/` file.
  - _The method._
    - The land boxes: the climate model's 2.5° × 5° boxes whose `EARTH_LAND_BOXES` share is at
      least 500 thousandths, 1,713 of 5,184. At level 7, 132 of them, all near the poles, hold no
      climate cell's centre and are left out: 1,581 are compared.
    - A model box: the solid-angle mean over the climate cells whose centres lie in it, land and
      sea, of each cell's sea-level temperature plus its month's anomaly, and of its month's
      precipitation; an observed box is likewise its whole area's mean (GPCP: the two 2.5° cells it
      spans; NCEP: the 2.5° points' cells, conservatively). The model's month m stands for calendar
      month m: its months are equal spans of eccentric anomaly from periapsis, which the test puts
      2.5 days after 1 January (`hydrology`'s `DJF_MONTHS`), and their midpoints in time lie +1.98
      to +3.87 days after the calendar months' (printed). Over the northern land, whose cycle
      changes by up to about 0.26 K a day in spring and autumn, that offset alone makes the
      model's March–April up to about 1 K colder, and its October–November as much warmer, than the
      calendar's would be; the monthly figures below are as compared, not corrected. Annual means
      weigh the months by their days (February 28.25), the model's alike.
    - The skill: the centred pattern correlation (Taylor 2001, J. Geophys. Res. 106(D7),
      7183–7192, eq. 1, not re-read here),
      the boxes weighted by solid angle; the same correlation of each field's departures from its
      mean over the band's land boxes (the structure in longitude, which the zonal tests do not
      hold); the RMS of the difference, its bias included; and the bias.
    - The signatures, each region the land boxes lying wholly within its bounds, chosen on the
      observed tables' land boxes before the model was run: the rain shadows as the lee's annual
      precipitation over the windward side's (the
      southern Andes under the westerlies, 37.5–50° S, the column 70–65° W over 75–70° W; the Sierra
      Nevada, 35–42.5° N, 120–115° W over 125–120° W; the Himalaya, the Tibetan Plateau at
      30–37.5° N over the Ganges's plain at 22.5–27.5° N, 75–90° E, also in JJA); the west
      coasts' deserts as the west coast's over the east coast's at the same latitudes (the Atacama,
      15–30° S, 75–65° W, over 50–40° W; the Namib, 17.5–30° S, 10–20° E, over 30–40° E; the Sonoran and Baja
      California, 22.5–32.5° N, 120–105° W, over the Gulf of Mexico's coast, 100–80° W; Western
      Australia, 20–32.5° S, 110–125° E, over 145–155° E); and the Indian monsoon as India's
      (10–25° N, 70–90° E) JJA over DJF, with its wettest month. The northern land's (30–70° N)
      seasonal cycle of temperature is printed with its first harmonic, each series fitted by least
      squares at its own months' midpoints. The boxes are coarse, so some regions hold a box of
      another climate: the Sonoran region the Sierra Madre's (26.25° N and 28.75° N, 110–105° W;
      GPCP 1.97 and 1.59 mm/day), "Tibet" the windward box at 31.25° N, 75–80° E (2.61), and the
      Atacama the Yungas' at 16.25° S, 70–65° W (3.52).
  - _Asserted_ (what the first run showed robust, each with a margin;
    `the_relief_earth_s_months_follow_gpcp_and_ncep_r1_its_lees_and_west_coasts_are_drier_and_india_is_wetter_in_jja`):
    every land box left out lies poleward of 70° (the least |latitude| of the 132, 78.75°); the

    `the_observed_tables_hold_earth_s_land_boxes_in_order`): the tables hold the 1,713 land boxes, once each, in order, the two alike, every value in range.
    Everything else is a figure, below.

- **Findings** (recorded; the model untuned, as the task rules).
  - _B1, the South Asian monsoon: a visibly wrong climate_ (reported to main as a blocker-class
    finding, the class "a visibly wrong picture"). India's JJA is 1.92 mm/day against GPCP's 8.17,
    its DJF 0.98 against 0.35, and the Ganges's plain (22.5–27.5° N, 75–90° E) gets 0.31 mm/day a
    year against 3.47 (113 mm against about 1,270): desert (BW) by Köppen–Geiger's bound at its
    temperatures (Peel et al. 2007, Table 1: MAP under 5 P_th, P_th = 2 MAT + 28 for a summer-heavy
    year, 390 mm at 25 °C) where Earth's is humid, with Tibet at 0.02 mm/day against 1.02. The
    heuristic's
    rain band follows the zonal energy-flux equator, capped, and its orographic model carries the
    sea's air along the bands by the wind's zonal part (Design note 8): nothing carries the
    monsoon's cross-equatorial south-westerlies onto the subcontinent. Ruling needed: whether a
    generated Earth-like world's monsoon coasts may read as desert (a model revision, which rides a
    bump) or stand as the heuristic's recorded limit.
  - _F1, the shadows and the west coasts are too weak._ Every rain shadow and west-coast desert has
    the right sign, but where the observed lee or west coast has a third of its wet side's rate, the
    model's has 0.52–0.84 of it (the Himalaya apart): its lee sides are too wet (Patagonia 3.27
    mm/day against 1.13, the Great Basin 2.51 against 0.81), its east coasts too dry (Brazil's
    1.51 against 3.78, the Gulf of Mexico's 2.03 against 3.30). The shadows' verdict mixes the
    heuristic with the test's relief: the synthetic sea gives each band's land its heights in the
    order of Earth2014's box medians, which leave no crest at 5°. By those medians the Andes'
    windward boxes stand at 0.61–0.89 km and their lee at 0.18–0.84 km (at 41.25° S the lee the
    higher), and the Sierra Nevada's "windward" boxes (0.32 and 1.29 km) below its lee (1.07–1.86
    km), so neither is a fair test of an orographic shadow; the Himalaya's (Tibet 1.7–5.1 km over
    the plain's 0.06–0.44 km) is.
  - _F2, the land is too dry_, by 0.40 mm/day in the annual mean over the land boxes (0.98 in
    July), most in the northern summer.
  - _F3, the land's seasonal cycle lags._ Over the northern land the model's cycle has NCEP's range
    (29.6 K against 29.0 K) but peaks 30.7 days late, each series dated at its own months'
    midpoints: over all the land boxes 3.9–4.3 K too cold in March–April and 1.6–2.4 K too warm in
    October–December as compared, of which the months' 2–4 day offset accounts for up to about
    1 K (the method). Its effective heat capacity over land, or the land's coupling to the sea in
    the geographic model, is the likely cause (not investigated).
  - _F4, the temperature's pattern in longitude is weak._ The departures from the bands' means
    correlate at −0.26 to +0.45 by month (annual −0.07): the zonal structure carries the
    temperature's high pattern correlation (0.82–0.98). The σ = 0.995 reference blurs the winter continents'
    inversions, so the winter figures are the less certain.

- **The regimes Earth cannot test: published GCM figures for the locked contrast's shape**
  (record-and-compare, not asserted; read 2026-10-30 from the arXiv preprints, Merlis and Schneider
  arXiv:1001.5117v1, Yang et al. arXiv:1404.4992v1 and Haqq-Misra et al. arXiv:1710.00435v4, the
  2020 revision; the pages are the preprints'; figures read off the vector graphics, calibrated to
  their axes, marked ±). T13.b's record sent the locked Earth's 124 K contrast here (340 K at the
  substellar point, 216 K at the antistellar, on a 24-day orbit, before T13.d imposes plan 14's
  contrast on P₁(cos γ)).
  - _Merlis and Schneider 2010_ (JAMES 2, 13; a gray-radiation moist GCM over a slab-ocean
    aquaplanet at 1,367 W m⁻²; Fig. 1, p. 4, 10 K contours; the day and night means not stated).
    At a 365-day rotation: the substellar point 300–310 K (±5), the antistellar 240–250 K (±5),
    the contours of 300, 290, 280, 270, 260 and 250 K at γ = 28.6°, 50.7°, 65.4°, 75.9°, 83.9° and
    89.9° (±1°), the terminator 250 K, the nightside 240–250 K throughout, the hottest point the
    substellar one, maximum less minimum 50–70 K. At a 1-day rotation: the substellar point about
    300 K, the field no longer a function of γ, warm lobes above 300 K at latitudes ±20–50° "near
    ∼40° latitude" (§4b, p. 11) reaching γ ≈ 108°, the nightside 240–280 K. Quoted: the surface
    temperature is "decreasing monotonically and isotropically away from the subsolar point; the
    night side of the planet has a nearly uniform surface temperature" (§3a, pp. 3–4); the night
    side "does not fall below ∼240 K" (abstract).
  - _Yang et al. 2014_ (ApJL 787, L2): no map of a locked world's field. Its one locked run (CAM3,
    rotation and orbit both 365 days) appears only as global means in its Fig. 1a (p. 2): 239.4 K
    with a 50 m slab, 241.4 K with 1 m, 265.0 K with clouds off (±0.5). No substellar, antistellar,
    day, night or terminator figure is given.
  - _Haqq-Misra et al. 2018_ (ApJ 852, 67; CAM, 1 bar N₂–H₂O, a swamp-ocean aquaplanet under
    M-dwarf spectra). Medians over 5° bins of γ from Fig. 7 (p. 29; ±1.5 K, its colour bands

    The hottest point lies within 3° of the substellar one for 4500–3700 K; "for slow rotators
    again the surface temperature maximum is found at the substellar point" (§5, p. 18); the night
    has "near-uniform cold temperatures across most of the hemisphere" (App. A, p. 20). Its regimes
    (abstract, §4): slow rotators, Rossby radius and Rhines length both above the radius, periods
    above about 20 days; Rhines rotators, the Rhines length below the radius and the Rossby radius
    above it, about 5–20 days; rapid rotators, the Rossby radius below the radius, under about 5
    days. Two misprints read there: p. 18's ">5 days" for the rapid rotators, and §4.1's "≈0.7"
    where Fig. 2 shows ΔT ÷ T_eq ≈ 0.07. At 3300 K the text's "beginnings of an eastward shift"
    (§4.3, p. 13) does not match the figure, whose hottest band lies about 15° west and 8° south.

  - _The shape they share_ (the reading lane's arithmetic on the figures above): on a slow rotator
    the dayside falls isotropically and monotonically from a maximum at the substellar point, far
    flatter than T_ss cos^¼ γ: by γ = 60° only 27–44% (Merlis and Schneider) or 34–38% (Haqq-Misra
    et al.'s slow cases) of the fall to the night has happened, the rest between 60° and about 110°,
    and Merlis and Schneider's 250 K contour stands at 90°, where cos^¼ γ would put it at 61–65°.
    The terminator lies 0–17% of the day–night contrast above the night, and the night is nearly
    uniform (within one 10 K contour; 7–9 K from 5% to 95% in Haqq-Misra et al.'s slow cases).
    Faster rotation breaks the symmetry: off-equatorial maxima (Merlis and Schneider at 1 day,
    Haqq-Misra et al. at 3000 K) and a night no longer monotonic in γ (3300 K: a 264 K terminator
    against a 275 K antistellar point). The reference locked Earth on its 24-day orbit falls in
    Haqq-Misra et al.'s slow regime, just past their 19.6-day case.
  - _To compare_ (not built here): the model's locked profile in the same terms (the share of the
    day–night fall reached by γ = 60°, the terminator's height above the night as a share of the
    contrast, the night's spread from 5% to 95%, and the hottest point's offset), printed by a
    locked world's test after T13.d's normalisation; then each figure beside the slow rotators'.

  postprocessing, 596 s in all; area-weighted mean surface temperatures 287.1 K and 286.8 K (180–317

  (`Model.configure(landmap=…, topomap=…)`), so "the same inputs" is expressible; its comparison is
  ambiguous at T21, which is why observations replaced it on Earth. Kept for a generated, non-Earth
  geography, if a GCM's view of one is ever wanted.
